# World Layoffs Data Analysis Using SQL

## 📜 Project Overview
This project analyzes global layoff data to uncover trends, patterns, and insights. The study focuses on identifying industries, companies, and regions most affected by layoffs, as well as the factors driving these trends.

---

## 📂 Dataset
- **File**: `layoffs.csv`
- **Details**: Contains information about layoffs, including company details, industry, location, and workforce statistics.

---

## 🛠️ Tools Used
- **SQL Server**: For data cleaning, transformation, and analysis.

---

## 🧹 Data Cleaning Process
Key steps in preparing the dataset for analysis:
1. **Duplicate Removal**: Eliminated duplicate records for consistency.
2. **Standardization**:
   - Unified industry names (e.g., 'Crypto' → 'Cryptocurrency').
   - Standardized country and company names.
   - Converted date fields to the proper date format.
3. **Handling Missing Values**:
   - Filled missing industries using self-joins.
   - Removed rows with null values in critical fields (e.g., `total_layoffs`).
4. **Validation**: Verified transformations to ensure accuracy.

---

## 🔍 Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)
Key insights derived from the analysis:
1. **Timeframe**: March 2020 to March 2023, covering significant global economic events like COVID-19.
2. **High-Impact Companies**:
   - Major layoffs by companies like Google, Meta, Amazon, and Microsoft.
   - Startups at early stages with 100% workforce layoffs.
3. **Geographical Impact**:
   - The United States faced the most layoffs, with notable city-level data.
4. **Yearly Trends**:
   - Significant spike in layoffs during 2022.
5. **Industry Analysis**:
   - Consumer and tech industries were hardest hit.
6. **Company Stage**:
   - Post-IPO companies accounted for the majority of layoffs.

---

## 📈 Key Findings
- **Economic Trends**: Layoffs were driven by economic and operational challenges.
- **Industry-Specific Impact**: Consumer-facing industries were significantly affected by changing shopping behaviors and supply chain issues.
- **Geographical Concentration**: The U.S., particularly tech hubs, saw the highest layoffs.
- **Layoff Scale**: Large-scale layoffs occurred predominantly in post-IPO companies.


## 🏆 Conclusion
This analysis sheds light on the global layoff landscape from 2020 to 2023. By understanding the trends and underlying causes, businesses and policymakers can take steps to mitigate the effects of future workforce reductions.
